This **three-polker coin**, known in Polish as a *półtorak*, was minted in **1624 CE** at **Bydgoszcz** during the reign of **Sigismund III Vasa (1587–1632)**, King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania. The coin was valued at *1½ grosz*, equivalent to three polker. It was part of a widely circulating series of small silver coins produced in substantial quantities during the early 17th century.

The **obverse** features the crowned coat of arms of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, combining the heraldic symbols of Poland and Lithuania with the dynastic arms of the Vasa family. The surrounding Latin inscription identifies Sigismund III and his royal titles. The **reverse** depicts a cross-topped imperial orb containing the abbreviated date, surrounded by an inscription identifying the coin as new money of the Kingdom of Poland.

## Historical Significance

The **Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth** was established in **1569** through the Union of Lublin, which united the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania into a single political federation. At its greatest extent, the Commonwealth was among the largest states in Europe, encompassing much of modern Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine, and neighboring territories. Its political system was unusual for the period. Rather than functioning as an absolute monarchy, it operated as an **elective monarchy**, in which the king was chosen by the nobility and governed alongside a powerful parliament known as the *Sejm*. The Commonwealth was also characterized by considerable religious, linguistic, and cultural diversity.

**Sigismund III Vasa** ruled the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth from **1587 to 1632** and was also King of Sweden from 1592 until his deposition in 1599. A member of the Swedish Vasa dynasty, Sigismund sought to strengthen royal authority and promote Catholicism during a period of intense religious and political competition in Europe. His reign was marked by prolonged conflicts with Sweden, Muscovy, and the Ottoman Empire. In **1596**, Sigismund transferred the principal royal residence and court from Kraków to Warsaw, contributing to Warsaw's emergence as the political center of the Commonwealth. By 1624, when this coin was minted, Sigismund had ruled for nearly four decades. His government faced the continuing financial and military demands of regional warfare.

Although Sigismund had been deposed as King of Sweden in 1599, he never renounced his claim, regarding his cousin **Gustavus Adolphus**, who inherited the Swedish crown in 1611, as an illegitimate ruler. Their rivalry was intensified by religious differences: Sigismund was a committed Catholic, while Gustavus Adolphus was a Lutheran monarch who increasingly positioned Sweden as a major Protestant power. By 1624, when C033 was minted, the two rulers had already fought over control of Livonia, a strategically important region encompassing parts of modern Latvia and Estonia. Gustavus Adolphus renewed his offensive against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in 1625, capturing important territories along the Baltic coast and eventually bringing the conflict into Prussia. Although Polish forces achieved notable victories, including at the Battle of Trzciana in 1629, Sweden secured advantageous trading rights in the Truce of Altmark later that year.

The conflict helped transform Sweden into a major Baltic power and weakened the Commonwealth's commercial position. Gustavus Adolphus subsequently turned his attention to the Thirty Years' War, intervening in Germany in 1630 and becoming one of Protestant Europe's most celebrated military commanders before his death at the Battle of Lützen in 1632. Sigismund died that same year, having never recovered the Swedish throne.
