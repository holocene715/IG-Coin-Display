This bronze coin was struck at **Antioch on the Orontes** during the reign of **Alexander II Zabinas**, one of several rival kings who contested control of the Seleucid Empire during the turbulent late second century BCE.

The obverse bears the diademed portrait of Alexander II, while the reverse depicts **Athena**, one of the most prominent deities inherited from the Greek religious and artistic tradition. Athena holds a small figure of **Nike**, the personification of victory, who extends a wreath. The Greek inscription identifies the issuing ruler as King Alexander.

## Historical Significance

**Alexander II Zabinas** came to power during a period of intense dynastic conflict within the declining Seleucid Empire. He emerged as a rival to **Demetrios II Nikator** and gained control of Antioch and significant portions of Syria, but his authority was short-lived. His reign illustrates how the once-enormous Seleucid state had fragmented into a succession of competing royal claims by the late second century BCE. Even control of the royal capital at Antioch could change hands as rival kings sought support from neighboring powers and local populations.

Despite this political instability, coins such as this demonstrate the remarkable persistence of **Hellenistic cultural traditions** in the Near East. More than two centuries after Alexander the Great's conquests, Seleucid rulers continued to use Greek language, royal portraiture, and deities such as Athena and Nike to communicate political legitimacy. The survival of this visual and linguistic tradition amid the empire's fragmentation illustrates how Hellenistic culture outlasted the political structures that had originally spread it across the region.
