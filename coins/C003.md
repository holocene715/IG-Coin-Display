This silver drachm was issued during the reign of **Antiochos VII Euergetes**, commonly known as **Antiochos VII Sidetes**, one of the last Seleucid kings to exercise substantial power beyond Syria. For this display, the coin is attributed to **Antioch on the Orontes**, the principal political center of the later Seleucid kingdom.

The obverse depicts the king wearing the royal diadem, while the reverse shows **Nike**, the Greek personification of victory, advancing left and carrying a wreath. The Greek inscription identifies the ruler as **King Antiochos Euergetes**, using an epithet meaning “Benefactor.”

## Historical Significance

**Antiochos VII Sidetes** presided over one of the last major attempts to restore the territorial power of the Seleucid Empire. After taking the throne during a period of dynastic instability, he campaigned successfully in the eastern Mediterranean and then launched a major expedition eastward against the **Parthian Empire**. His forces temporarily recovered substantial portions of Mesopotamia and territories farther east that had previously been lost by the Seleucids.

The restoration was short-lived. Antiochos was killed during renewed fighting with the Parthians in **129 BCE**, after which Seleucid authority contracted increasingly toward Syria. His death therefore marks an important stage in the transfer of regional power from the Hellenistic Seleucid state to the expanding **Parthian Empire**. This makes his reign particularly relevant to a collection focused on Central Asia and the Near East: it represents one of the final large-scale efforts by a successor state of Alexander the Great to reassert control over the eastern territories of the Hellenistic world.
