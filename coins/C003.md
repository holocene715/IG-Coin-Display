This silver drachm was issued during the reign of **Antiochos VII Euergetes**, commonly known as **Antiochos VII Sidetes**, one of the last Seleucid kings to exercise substantial power beyond Syria. The obverse depicts the king wearing the royal diadem, while the reverse shows **Nike**, the Greek personification of victory, advancing left and carrying a wreath. The Greek inscription identifies the ruler as **King Antiochos Euergetes**, using an epithet meaning “Benefactor.”

The Seleucid Empire was a vast kingdom founded by one of Alexander the Great’s generals after Alexander’s death in 323 BCE. At its height, it stretched from the eastern Mediterranean into Central Asia, bringing Greek traditions into contact with many older cultures. Its territory gradually shrank as rival powers rose, and Rome took control of its remaining lands in 64 BCE.

## Historical Significance

**Antiochos VII Sidetes** presided over one of the last major attempts to restore the territorial power of the Seleucid Empire. After taking the throne during a period of dynastic instability, he campaigned successfully in the eastern Mediterranean and then launched a major expedition eastward against the **Parthian Empire** (see coins C006 and C007). His forces temporarily recovered substantial portions of Mesopotamia and territories farther east that had previously been lost by the Seleucids.

The restoration was short-lived. Antiochos was killed during renewed fighting with the Parthians in **129 BCE**, after which Seleucid authority contracted increasingly toward Syria. His death therefore marks an important stage in the transfer of regional power from the Hellenistic Seleucid state to the expanding **Parthian Empire** (see coins C006 and C007). This makes his reign particularly relevant to a collection focused on Central Asia and the Near East: it represents one of the final large-scale efforts by a successor state of Alexander the Great to reassert control over the eastern territories of the Hellenistic world.
