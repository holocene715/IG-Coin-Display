This small silver **2 soldi coin** was issued by the **Republic of Venice** for circulation in the **Levant**, the eastern Mediterranean region with which Venetian merchants maintained extensive commercial relationships. The coin was authorized by decree on **September 12, 1565**, during the dogeship of **Girolamo Priuli (in power 1559–1567)**, and continued to be produced under later doges until approximately 1600. The **obverse** bears the **winged Lion of Saint Mark**, the emblem of Venice, with a paw resting upon the Gospels. The surrounding inscription, **SANCTVS MARCVS VENET**, identifies the city's patron saint. The **reverse** depicts **Saint Justina** holding a sword and scales, flanked by lions. Its inscription, **DILIGITE IVSTITIAM**, means **“Love justice.”**

## Historical Significance

By the 16th century, the **Republic of Venice** had been a leading Mediterranean maritime and commercial power for centuries. Its merchants connected the markets of northern Italy and central Europe with ports across the Adriatic, Aegean, and eastern Mediterranean. Goods moving through these networks included spices, textiles, silk, metals, and other valuable commodities. Venice governed not only the lagoon city itself but also mainland territories in northern Italy and an extensive, geographically scattered collection of overseas colonies. In 1565, these included **Crete, Cyprus, Corfu, and parts of Dalmatia**, alongside other islands, ports, and coastal strongholds. The republic's commercial influence reached considerably farther than the territory it directly administered.

The eastern Mediterranean was also an arena of intense competition with the **Ottoman Empire**. Venice sought to preserve access to eastern markets even as Ottoman expansion challenged its military and territorial position. Trade and diplomacy therefore continued alongside periodic warfare. This 2 soldi coin is particularly revealing because it was specifically intended for circulation in the **Levant**. Its existence reflects the need for currency that could function within commercial networks spanning multiple political jurisdictions and monetary traditions.

A **soldo** was a unit within the Venetian lira system, with twenty soldi conventionally accounting for one lira. The 2-soldi denomination represented a comparatively small payment, unlike the high-value gold ducats for which Venice was also famous. 

The **winged Lion of Saint Mark** was among the most recognizable symbols of Venice. According to Venetian tradition, the relics of Saint Mark the Evangelist had been brought to the city from Alexandria in 828 CE. The saint became Venice's patron and a central emblem of its civic and political identity. The lion appears on Venetian flags, public buildings, official seals, and coins. 

The date **1565** places the introduction of this coin shortly before a major change in Venetian power in the eastern Mediterranean. In **1570**, the Ottoman Empire invaded **Cyprus**, then under Venetian rule. The ensuing war culminated in the famous **Battle of Lepanto in 1571**, in which a coalition including Venice defeated an Ottoman fleet. Despite that naval victory, Venice relinquished Cyprus under the peace settlement of **1573**. The 2-soldi type coin began circulating at a moment when Venice still held Cyprus but faced growing Ottoman pressure. Its continued production into later decades crossed a period of major territorial and diplomatic change.
