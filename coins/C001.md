This bronze coin was struck at **Antioch on the Orontes**, one of the principal cities and mints of the Seleucid Empire, during the joint rule of **Cleopatra Thea and her son Antiochos VIII Grypos**.

The radiate portrait of Antiochos VIII occupies the obverse, while the reverse depicts an **owl standing on an amphora**. The surrounding Greek inscription names both Cleopatra and Antiochos, reflecting their joint authority during this period.

The coin dates to the final decades of the Seleucid Empire, when dynastic disputes increasingly divided a kingdom that had once extended across much of the Near East and Central Asia.

## Historical Significance

The reign of **Antiochos VIII Grypos** belongs to the final, increasingly fragmented period of the Seleucid Empire. By the late second century BCE, the Seleucid state had lost much of the enormous territory once controlled by its early rulers, but it remained an important political and cultural power in Syria and the eastern Mediterranean. Antiochos VIII's joint rule with his mother, **Cleopatra Thea**, reflects the intense dynastic politics of this period. Cleopatra had already exercised power alongside several Seleucid kings and became one of the most prominent royal women of the Hellenistic world. Their partnership eventually ended violently when Antiochos VIII asserted sole rule.

More broadly, this period illustrates the gradual transformation of the **Hellenistic Near East** following the conquests of Alexander the Great. Even as Seleucid political power weakened, Greek language, artistic traditions, institutions, and coinage remained deeply embedded across the region and interacted with older local cultures. Antioch, where this coin was struck, remained one of the great cities of the eastern Mediterranean long after the Seleucid dynasty disappeared, later becoming a major center of the Roman and early Christian worlds. Coins such as this therefore come from a politically unstable period whose cultural legacy proved far more durable than the empire itself.
